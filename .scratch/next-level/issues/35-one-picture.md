# One picture, at the screen's own resolution, fast on weak machines

Status: open

Spec: `../spec.md`

The owner's call (2026-11-10): "Keep only 1 setting, full 100% render, looks ugly as fuck to have lower, and optimize the game's performance so it can look absolutely stunning while running on potato PC's."

Ticket 22's quality tiers, Auto, dynamic resolution and High's 3.7 MP pixel cap are gone. Every machine draws the one picture: the old High (2048 px shadows over 45 m at 60 Hz, GTAO at half resolution with 8 samples, temporal AA with a light sharpen, sun shafts, bloom, heat shimmer, 8x texture filtering, full grass, scatter and particles), always at the window's own pixels (the device pixel ratio, held at 2 at most: a 3x phone draws 2x).

Phase 1 (v2.166.0): the picture fixed. Phase 2 and on: make that picture cheap enough for weak PCs, measured with `?bench` and `?benchmark` (frame time, GPU time, draw calls), each gain shipped as its own release.

## Comments

**Phase 1 (v2.166.0).** Removed: `QUALITY`, the tiers, Auto (`TierSearch`, `tierFromHints`, the remembered `autoTier`/`autoAdapter`), `DynamicResolution`, the pixel cap and `?megapixels`, `?tier`, `&dynres`, `duty.setTier`, the FXAA path for lower tiers, the HUD's "% res", the Quality section and the Dynamic resolution toggle in Settings > Video, and the tier and dynamic-resolution choices on the benchmark page. An old save carrying `quality`, `autoTier`, `autoAdapter` or `dynamicResolution` loads and forgets them. Settings > Video keeps the frame-rate cap, the FPS counter, the field of view and the benchmark link, and its preview now lists what the one picture draws.

The frame-rate cap now runs in `FramePacer` (`quality.ts`). The reviewer found that its 60-second re-read of the display's rate would take a slow machine on a 144 Hz screen (ticked every other refresh) for a 72 Hz display; it is now read again only when the window moves or the screen under it changes (`displayMoved`), with a test of a slow machine held for five minutes.

Verified: typecheck; the fast suite (177 files, 2318 tests); the canvas is 1920x1080 at DPR 1, 2880x1800 at DPR 2, 780x1688 for a 390x844 phone at DPR 3; Settings > Video at desktop and phone width (`one-picture/video-*.png`), with an old Low + dynamic-resolution save loaded, no console errors.

**Phase 2a (v2.167.0): instanced matrices.** On a weak processor the frame is the processor's, not the GPU's: at 1080p this PC's GPU draws a frame in 1.3 to 1.8 ms, while the processor slowed four times (`one-picture/potato.mjs`, CDP CPU throttling) takes 20 to 40 ms. A profile of Zero Island at that speed found three.js uploading every instanced mesh's matrices again for every pass of every frame (an instanced mesh of up to 1024 instances keeps them in a uniform buffer whose `update()` is always true), and, with temporal AA's velocity drawn, copying each one's whole matrix array to last frame's after every pass. `apps/range/three-patch/plugin.ts` patches three's WebGPU build as it is bundled: a matrix buffer is uploaded only when its attribute's `version` moved, and copied to last frame's only when it changed. A pattern missing after a three upgrade fails the build (`three-patch.test.ts` checks both builds). Reviewed by a subagent against three's binding and buffer code: per-object bindings, so no pass can mark another's buffer as sent; every matrix writer in the game sets `needsUpdate`.

Throttled x4, 1080p, `?bench` flight (mean frame / 1% low): Zero Island 39.8 ms / 548 ms before, 35.5 ms / 485 ms after; Survival Town 20.9 ms / about 54 ms before, 19.8 ms / about 54 ms after.

**Phase 2b (v2.168.0): the sky only where it shows.** A weak GPU's frame is the world pass: at 4K (DPR 2, the most pixels the game draws) on Survival Town's cul-de-sac it was 5.3 to 5.7 ms of an 8 ms frame, with post (GTAO 0.85, TRAA 0.54, bloom 0.4, shafts and the screen pass 0.7) cheap beside it, and about 80% of it shading (every material swapped for one flat one: 1.06 ms). Three.js drew the scene's background first and without a depth test, so every pixel ran the cloudy sky's shader before the world covered most of them. Two more patches in `three-patch/plugin.ts` draw it after the opaque world and before what is see-through, with the depth test on: it sits on the far plane, so it fills only the pixels nothing drew, and glass, smoke and particles still blend over it.

Separate runs could not show it: the GPU's clocks and this PC's other load moved a spot's total by 30% between runs, more than the gain. `one-picture/gpu-toggle.mjs` measures in one page instead, switching the sky off and on every 2.5 s and adding each pass's GPU time by the state it was drawn in. The sky's cost at 4K, before: 1.24 ms on Survival Town's cul-de-sac (8.73 ms frame against 7.45 without a sky), 0.93 ms on Switchyard's long lane west (7.26 against 6.23); after: none measurable (7.29 against 7.47). Same picture: every reference spot of the five maps shot before and after (`apps/range/scripts/spots.mjs`, and Zero Island again with a longer wait since its first shots came before the island streamed in) differs only by TRAA jitter and wind. Reviewed by a subagent against three's render list: the background goes in after the sort, last of the opaque list; every material in the game that skips depth writes is transparent.

Measured and dropped: the three point lights (the muzzle and frag flashes, dark until something fires) cost nothing measurable switched off (`gpu-toggle.mjs lights`). `?stats` now also writes the GPU time per frame (`gpu`), for scripted measurements of a production build.

**Phase 2c (v2.169.0): depth first.** Most of the world pass's shading went on pixels that were then covered: the models of a map square are merged into one mesh, and a finish's walls into one across the map, so they draw in no useful order (Survival Town's bus lit every seat behind its sides). `depthFirst` (`map-models.ts`) gives each merged model mesh, and each static surface mesh but glass and lamps, a twin that writes only depth (no colour, a hair farther by polygon offset), drawn first of the opaque world (`renderOrder` -1); each pixel is then lit once. Measured in one page with `gpu-toggle.mjs depthfirst` at 4K, world pass with twins against without: Survival Town cul-de-sac 2.92 against 4.80 ms, its south living room 2.00 against 3.34, Station Nine bunker 3.70 against 4.47, Switchyard long lane west 2.45 against 3.10, Zero Island Ground Zero 5.95 against 6.12. The processor slowed four times shows no difference (`still.mjs`, twins shown and hidden, interleaved: Zero Island p50 32.1/33.2 against 30.7/33.1 ms, Survival Town 16.7/18.5 against 17.7/18.4). Every reference spot of the five maps matches its shot before (one blank Switchyard shot retaken). Reviewed by a subagent against three's sort, depth bias and MRT write mask; on its findings the twins are skipped when a world or the train is freed (the depth material is shared by every world, and freeing it would recompile the next world's twins on its first frame), and lamps get none.

Left for later: the terrain and plants (Zero Island's cost, where the twins gain least), and a weak-GPU check on real hardware.

**Phase 2d (v2.170.10): the ground branches.** Zero Island's ground, measured at 4K by swapping its shader for a flat colour in one page (`gpu-toggle.mjs groundflat`), cost 0.4 to 1.2 ms of a 5 to 7 ms GPU frame, and its parts (each taken out in turn against the full shader, over the menu's overview): lighting 0.43 ms, the relief's reads 0.29, the hexagons' extra reads 0.28, the cliff's two reads 0.22 (paid on every pixel, flat or not), the procedural noise 0.14; receiving the sun's shadow nothing measurable. `layeredGround` now builds its colour in an `Fn` that branches: the photos and relief are read only where the ground isn't sheer cliff, the cliff only where it is steep, and a hexagon only where it weighs more than one part in 255 (the weights read are renormalised); the reads in branches take their gradients from outside them. Against the old shader in one page (`gpu-toggle.mjs groundalt` with a temporary hook building the old one): Ground Zero 0.12 ms less, The Dustlands 0.15 and 0.60 in two runs, Mutant Jungle 0.06, Frostbite Peaks none; no spot slower. Same picture: Frostbite Peaks shot with each in one page (`ground-ab.mjs`) differs only where the wind moves plants and shadows and the clouds drift. The ground with the new shader still costs 0.69 ms at The Dustlands and 0.37 at Frostbite Peaks; its lighting is the biggest part left. Note for measuring: `?spot=` takes the map's own spot names (Zero Island's are its Regions' names, "Ground Zero"); an unknown name leaves the menu's overview up, which is what the first round of these numbers was taken over.
