## Ticket 23: a phone can play

Today a phone reaches a match with no way to move, aim or fire (audit F01, high). Build touch controls that a person would actually choose to play with, not a checkbox:

- A floating move stick on the left half (appears where the thumb lands; full deflection sprints, or a sprint lock at the top of its travel), look by dragging anywhere on the right half with sensible acceleration and its own sensitivity setting, a fire button that can also be dragged to aim while firing, aim (ADS), jump, crouch/slide, reload, swap weapon, grenade, melee, the interact/pick-up action Battle Royale needs, ping, scoreboard and pause. Every target at least 44x44 CSS px with 8 px gaps, inside the safe area (`env(safe-area-inset-*)`), laid out for both thumbs in landscape; in portrait either a usable layout or a plain "turn your phone" notice (decide, and say why).
- **Auto-fire as an option** (fires when the crosshair is on an enemy within the weapon's range, with a short delay; on by default on touch, a setting to turn it off) and a gentle aim slowdown over targets for touch (client side only: it must not send anything a mouse player could not send, and it must be off for mouse). The setting lives with the other settings and is stored the same way.
- Multi-touch done right: each control tracks its own pointer id (Pointer Events), nothing breaks when a third finger lands, the page never scrolls, zooms, selects text or shows the callout menu, and audio unlocks on the first touch. Fullscreen and orientation lock are requested where the browser allows them, on the player's own press.
- The HUD on touch: nothing sits under a thumb control; key hints ("Press F", "Tab") become their touch equivalents everywhere they appear (prompts, the pause menu's Controls list, the loadout banner, the Preparation and Battle Royale prompts such as jump/deploy, revive, pick up, ping). The pause menu, Settings, lobby, loadout pick, scoreboard, map and the Profile screen must all be usable by touch at 390x844 and 844x390.
- Touch is shown when the last input was a touch (a laptop with a touchscreen and a mouse switches both ways), never by sniffing the user agent.

The input reaches the simulation the same way the keyboard, mouse and pad do (`packages/fps-client/src/input.ts`, `gamepad.ts`, `usercmd`): put the touch layer in its own module(s) beside them with a small interface, and keep `input.ts`'s changes to wiring it in.
