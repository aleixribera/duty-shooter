Duty Shooter UI Directions

Round two, after your note: dark and low-lit. Every screen sits in shadow and only what matters gives off light: the option you are on, the button to press, the number that just changed, the kill. Five ways to draw that light, on the same five screens over real frames from the build. Pick one, or parts of several.

Light is the reward

At rest the HUD sits in low light: grey labels, white numbers, no glow. Light means something is chosen or something happened. A kill is the brightest moment on screen, then it fades back:

  • 0 msHit marker and a dry tick
  • 90 msThe marker turns the kill colour and glows, with a heavier thunk
  • 120 ms+150 lights up by the crosshair
  • 300 msThe name, and +50 for the headshot
  • 750 msA medal lights, only when earned; one at a time
  • 1.8 sBack to low light

Progression in three places

In the menu, your rank and the next unlock in the profile corner, and a lit dot on Loadouts when something new is waiting. In the fight, only score. After the match, one bar that sweeps and flashes as it rolls over the rank, two lines for Mastery and a Challenge, and one lit card for what you unlocked, with Equip it.

Toaster-safe

The glow is a box or text shadow on flat HTML: painted once, then only composited, so it costs the integrated GPU nothing worth measuring. Nothing blurs the game picture behind a panel; round one's frosted glass is gone. Light that animates fades in opacity, never a growing blur.

The menu darkens the world behind it so the selection is the brightest thing. In play the world can't be darkened, so there the light is only on the clock, your feed line and the kill. Press Play on the kill moment and the results to watch the timing.

My read

The default answer is Spotlight: the round-one Default you liked, turned down, with cyan light doing the selecting. It would be fine, and it is the safest.

I would go one step further: Spotlight's menus and screens, with Lit Panel's way of lighting the one button that matters (Play, Equip it, Play again) as a solid lit block, so the next action is never in doubt. In play, keep Edge Light's HUD: no boxes over the fight, so the only light in a fight is the kill.

What would prove me wrong: if the panel-less HUD reads poorly over the bright desert when you play, the HUD keeps Spotlight's dark panels instead.

Research behind it

  • VALORANT shaders and gameplay clarity (Riot): clarity first, on any hardware.
  • Caliber, our approach to HUD design: check every element over light, dark and mixed backgrounds.
  • Modern Warfare (2019) UI, from an Infinity Ward UI artist: a minimal gunsmith built around the attachments.
  • Why Modern Warfare 3's UI went blue (GamesRadar): the accent colour is a readability choice.
  • Apex Legends and visual clutter (NME): even the angular plates need restraint.
  • 7 beginner HUD mistakes, from a UI/UX art director.
  • Reward and level-up juice: bars that sweep rather than jump, a pulse at the rollover.
  • Warzone's rewards screen needs to chill (Kotaku): one reward card, not a flood.
  • Valorant's damage and kill feedback changes: players notice when feedback is taken away.
  • Black Ops 6 progression: account rank and weapon levels kept apart.
