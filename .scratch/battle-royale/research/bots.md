# Battle Royale bots: what the best games do, and what ours should change

For ticket 64. Researched 2026-11-03. Sources are listed at the end, and every claim carries a number like [3]. Where a commercial BR has published nothing (Apex, Warzone, Naraka, The Finals), this says so and leans on talks from studios that did publish.

## What the field knows

**About BR bots specifically, little has been published.** Fortnite, PUBG and Apex added bots to onboard new players. The better you are, the fewer you meet, and ranked has none [1][2][3][4]. Warzone keeps them out of its main mode [5]. The one public lesson comes from PUBG: update 12.1 "improved bot AI", players called the results "terminator bots", and two weeks later PUBG cut their accuracy, slowed their reactions and shortened their full-auto range [6]. **Players forgive slow aim, never inhuman speed.** The useful designs come from shooters whose bots stand in for people: Killzone 3, Counter-Strike, Left 4 Dead (L4D), Halo Infinite and Paragon [7][8][9][10][11].

**Architecture.** The pattern is commander, then squad, then individual: orders flow down and threat reports flow up. Squads route on a clustered strategic graph with influence-map costs. Individuals choose their own targets and positions, and may ignore orders to survive [7]. Paragon used one parameterised behaviour tree for every hero, scored positions with a 5 m influence grid (320 kB for the whole map) and scripted only the opening [11]. Halo Infinite weighs the valid actions against each other [10]. At our scale, which decision loop we use matters less than **knowledge shared across the squad** and **a cheap grid for scoring positions**.

**Perception.** Splinter Cell Blacklist separates perceiving from being aware. It casts rays to 8 bones, and the number that must be visible depends on stance. A detection timer then scales with distance and light. At range a box replaces the cone, so a figure far off to one side goes unseen [12]. Killzone's limited sensors mean that "the bot's idea of the world may be believably wrong" [7]. Hunt: Showdown makes gunshots audible across the whole map and uses dogs and crows as "sound traps" [13].

**Reaction and aim.** A person's simple reaction is about 0.2 s and a go/no-go choice about 0.4 s, before any aiming, and both are slower against a distant figure [14]. The Counter-Strike bot was built on "human limits such as reaction time and attention" [8]. The BotPrize winner, judged human 52% of the time, lost accuracy on fast turns and at long range, and held grudges [15]. Ocio Barriales sets difficulty by *when a hit may land*. A shared timer lets one shooter at a time hit a given target, and the gap shrinks when the target is close, in the open or charging. The other rounds miss *where the target sees them*: the top of the cover, the dirt at the peek side, past the face [16].

**Pacing and cost.** L4D's director tracks each survivor's intensity. It builds up, holds the peak for 3-5 s, then relaxes for 30-45 s, because constant combat is fatiguing [9]. Difficulty adjustment works best unseen [17]. Detail should go where the player would notice its absence (what they see, hear and remember), not simply where things are near [18]. Squad corridors and grid lookups keep the searches cheap [7][11].

## Gap analysis against our code

Already strong: blurred memory, human aim lag and wobble (`bot.ts:624-667`), cover spots, danger spots, soft cover, hiding and flanking (`bot.ts:1295-1370`), Heat, and ticket 40's finish delay. The gaps:

1. **The Battle Royale nerf goes to the wrong place.** `royaleProfile` (`profile.ts:192`) multiplies reaction by 2.6 and aim error by 3.1 (`stats.ts:364-365`). Reaction becomes 0.73-1.25 s plus up to 0.37 s of notice (`bot.ts:1127-1134`), 2-5x a human's [14], so bots visibly stand and stare. Initial error becomes 9-15°, and the misses are random wobble, not shots placed where the target sees them [16]. That is PUBG's lesson reversed: the slowness is visible.
2. **Hunting knows too much and hears too little.** Hunters go to the true position of the nearest enemy seat within 260 m, blurred by 12 m (`squad-plan.ts:229-241, 268`). That is a wallhack. Meanwhile bots hear gunfire only up to 40 m, or 22 m muffled (`bot.ts:88-89, 1160`). A person hears it up to 400 m (`net/relevance.ts:36`), so people can third-party by ear and bots can't [13].
3. **Squadmates don't share what they see.** Memory is per bot (`#memory`). Only kills become callouts (`bot.ts:1207`), and threat reports never travel up the chain [7].
4. **Detection is binary.** One clear ray to the head or chest, inside a 50° half-angle, gives a full target after reaction time (`bot.ts:1081-1104`). Distance, stance, movement and dusk light matter only through `notice`. A still, crouched player at 60 m is spotted like a sprinting one [12].
5. **No positional threat picture.** Rallies are random points inside the next circle (`squad-plan.ts:100, 154-169`), ignoring where fights and squads are. Rotation timing is good (`SPARE`, line 102), but the route and the edge chosen are not.
6. **No pacing per squad.** Heat paces the whole field, but a squad that just finished a fight can be hunted again at once [9].
7. **Detail by distance only.** `farBots` thins out thinking beyond 150 m of a person (`scale.ts:21`, `server.ts:1486`), so a bot 200 m away firing into a player's fight gets less detail than a quiet one at 120 m [18].
8. **New rules the bots don't know yet.** Anchors still pick the sniper by weight 5 (`profile.ts:154`), but ticket 71 takes it out of the loadout. There is no plan for Reboot Cards or Beacons (ticket 70), supply drops (71) or pings (66).

## How bots should handle Reboot, supply drops and pings

- **Reboot Cards and Beacons (ticket 70).** In `SquadPlanner`, a lying Card is a job like `#revives` (`squad-plan.ts:302`). The nearest standing bot takes it if it can arrive within the Card's 90 s, the Card lies inside `wallAt(t + run)`, and no enemy was seen near it in the last ~5 s. The carrier routes to the Beacon with the lowest cost that is off cooldown and inside the next circle, scoring candidates on the threat grid (change 5). It channels with the existing `use` objective (`bot.ts:65`). The other squadmates take an **overwatch** order: cover spots near the Beacon, facing the last known threats. No carrier goes after the last Stages that allow Reboot. Other bots hear the channel as they hear gunfire, which draws a third squad in. A human carrying a Card leads (`toLeader`, line 283), and bots escort them.
- **Supply drops (ticket 71).** Each drop is a commander-level assignment, like `planOutposts`' crowding cost (`planner.ts:28`). At most one or two squads commit. Value rises with nearness, being inside the next circle, squad health and Heat, and is higher for anchor and roamer styles. Other squads within earshot may take a watching spot at 40-70 m and third-party. Opening it uses the 2 s `use` objective. A bot that holds the sniper uses its existing scoped tactics and `longSightlines` (`nav-queries.ts:25`). Remove the sniper from Battle Royale bot weights, so that anchors carry the M4A1.
- **Pings (ticket 66).** A human's ping becomes a callout in each squad bot's memory: `seen: false`, at the pinged spot, with the `#marked` blur (`bot.ts:1222-1229`). "Going here" overrides the squad's rally. "Enemy" sets `#chasing` or holding facing by caution. Bots ping for humans: the first sighting of a new enemy after reaction time, rate-limited to one ping per squad every 3 s, plus "Going here" when the planner sends them to a drop or Beacon. In squads with no human, the same messages pass internally and never touch the network.

## Ranked changes for our game

| # | Change | Expected effect | Server-cost risk |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Move the nerf from reaction to visible misses.** Set `botReaction` to ~1.3 (0.36-0.62 s base). Add a **hit window per target** for every target, people and bots alike. Inside it, only one shooter's rounds may land, and the delay follows distance, stance, cover, facing and motion [16]. Other rounds aim at the cover top, the ground at the peek side or past the face, which ticket 65's tracers make visible. Retune `botAim` and the window in the pace soak for 1-in-4. | Bots react like people, and the advantage still comes from them missing in plain view rather than freezing. It also stops several squads killing one target in a single frame (story 57) without targeting humans differently. | Negligible: one timer per target and a little maths per shot. Balance retune needed. |
| 2 | **Hunt by ear, not by position.** Shots carry up to the 400 m a person hears (the bot gets a bearing and a distance band; muffled, about half that). Build a list of heard fights for each squad each tick. Hunters go to heard fights, and `#hunted` stops reading true positions. | Real third-parties and distant gunfire that leads somewhere (story 53). No wallhacking. | Low: fights × squads, not shots × bots. Cluster shots into 25 m cells first. |
| 3 | **Share squad knowledge.** Sightings and heard positions go to squadmates with callout blur, and the same channel carries pings (66). | Squads turn together and flank the same enemy. Bot squadmates feel like teammates for a human. | Very low. |
| 4 | **Detection meter.** Visible points needed by stance, a timer scaled by distance, motion, light and soft-cover thinning, and a box at range instead of a cone [12]. | Hiding, crouching and slow flanks work (stories 85-87). Long-range spotting becomes rarer, which also suits the sightline goals. | Low: rays we already cast, plus one float per pair. |
| 5 | **Threat grid for rotations.** One shared 25 m grid (1,600 cells) of decaying noise, deaths and sightings that every bot could plausibly know. Rallies and routes favour cold cells. Rally style by profile: anchors take the next circle's edge, rushers go deep. It uses the next-Ring preview (68). | Varied, believable rotations. Fewer squads walk into an ongoing fight by accident, and more choose to. | Low: Paragon-sized [11]. |
| 6 | **Reboot, supply drops and pings as above** (70, 71, 66). | Bots play the new rules, and Reboot doesn't favour humans. | Low. Drop assignment is once a drop. |
| 7 | **A breather after each fight, for every squad.** A squad that ends a fight is not a hunt target for 30-45 s, applied to bot squads and people alike [9]. A third-party during the fight is still allowed. | Spiky pacing: peaks, then a breather to reload and revive. | None. |
| 8 | **Think rate by relevance, not 150 m.** A bot runs at full rate if a person could see it, hear its shots, or is fighting it. Everything else thinks every 4 ticks [18]. | Fewer visible glitches near humans, and less work spent on bots nobody can perceive. | Lowers average cost. The worst case stays at the current 60-seat gate. |
| 9 | **Skill rises across the match** (optional, measure). Aim error falls ~10% a Stage after Stage 3, which stands in for real lobbies, where the survivors are the better players. | Late fights feel earned. | None. It shifts the 1-in-4 target, so tune it with #1. |

Measure each change in `ROYALE_PACE` over seeds 1-5, before and after: match length, Downs and Revives, Ring deaths, how many squads' fights get a third squad within 30 s, time to first fight, and the good-player proxy's win rate.
