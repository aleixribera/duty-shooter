Client (v2.17.0): presets retuned around Medium. Each preset now has a pixel budget (Low 1080p at one pixel per CSS pixel with FXAA and 30 Hz shadow redraws, Medium up to 1440p's worth on high-DPI screens with 60 Hz shadows, High supersampled 1.5x up to 4K's worth), and the loading screen compiles every viewmodel weapon, the frag, and every soldier look before the match starts. Pipeline probe: 279 pipelines compile on the menu and loading screen, 16 are left in play (a few shadow variants, the muzzle flash, frag and grenade pieces). A fair A/B of Medium at 1080p (same content, alternating builds) is a wash: p50 8–9 ms both. Both builds still have multi-second frames in queue submit and writeBuffer mid-match on this PC, which was saturated by other sessions' headless Chromes during the runs. Follow-up: warm the last 16 pipelines and re-measure the stalls on an idle PC.

v2.18.0: the warm-up frame also draws a frag, a scope glint, every particle and decal layer, the casings and both muzzle flashes, and redraws the shadow map, so their shaders compile too. Pipeline probe over a 6v6 Bot Match with firing, a weapon switch and a frag: the build before this work compiled 12 pipelines in play, this one 0 (310 before the match starts). Low's FXAA path drew a black world (the render-to-texture FXAA reads through resets the clear colour to opaque, so the viewmodel layer covered everything); the viewmodel scene now resets its transparent clear as its pass starts, and Low, Medium and High screenshots are checked. Frame timing on this PC could not separate the builds: it sat at 63% CPU from other apps during the runs, and the same build measured p50 7 ms in one run and 74 ms in another. Both builds still show 9–14 frames over 500 ms per 30 s run in queue submit and writeBuffer, with no pipeline compiled in play, so those stalls are GPU-process contention on this machine, not shader compiles; measure them on an idle PC before treating them as a game bug.

v2.19.0: the multi-second freezes in the first ~20 s of play were the browser still compiling the ~300 shaders created behind the menu and the loading screen. Chrome compiles them in its GPU process in the background, and the first frames that needed the GPU waited on it: a GPU trace showed a 10 KB writeBuffer blocking 5.1 s and a submit 5.9 s, and an explore walk with no bots froze the same way, only in its first 15 s. The loading screen now waits (20 s at most) until the GPU has finished the frames drawn so far. Alternating the builds on this PC: a 6v6 Bot Match went from 10 frames over 500 ms (worst 7.1 s) to 1 (worst 0.54 s); an explore walk from 5 long frames after the map was ready (worst 9.4 s, about 22 s frozen in all) to 1 (worst 0.46 s), in each of two runs. The cost is a longer loading screen, about 30 s more on this loaded PC with below-normal-priority test browsers and an empty shader cache; Chrome keeps compiled shaders on disk, so a returning player's loads are shorter. A first-play stall in an unprimed browser is therefore expected on the loading screen, not in play. Also seen: the first frame of play makes about 20 copies of the same shadow shader (one per practice dummy); Chrome deduplicates identical code, so they are cheap.

v2.20.1, Medium's CPU per frame. With GPU timestamps a Medium 1080p frame takes about 0.6 ms of GPU on the RTX 5060, so Medium is bound by the CPU (and on a high-DPI screen v2.17.0's pixel budget already took GPU time from 3.07 to 0.97 ms and frames from 14.6 to 5.5 ms). A CPU profile of a Medium frame (explore, standing, 10 s) put 5.2 of 6.6 ms in three.js rendering and 0.52 ms in the audio listener: nine AudioParam writes a frame, each a message to the audio thread. The listener now writes only the values that moved (never `up`). Map models are merged into one mesh per material within 160 m squares instead of one instanced mesh per asset part (Survival Town: 148 instanced meshes to 7, 382 draw calls in a shadow frame to 349). Old and new builds alternated: Survival Town 6.60 and 5.92 ms of CPU a frame to 4.44 and 5.21 (154 and 172 fps to 227 and 196), Switchyard 4.99 to 4.23 ms (203 to 239 fps). 48 m squares were tried first and left as many meshes as before.
